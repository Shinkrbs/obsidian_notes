------
**What is data?**
- Raw, recorded observations or measurements collected about variables of interest
- Data become information only after they are organized, processed and interpreted in context

**Why data type matters**
- Determines which summary statistics are meaningful (mean vs mode)
- Determine which charts are appropriate (histogram vs bar chart)
- Determine which hypothesis tests can be applied (t-test vs chi-square)
- Applying the wrong tool to the wrong data type produces invalid results

**Two Top-Level Types**
- *Quantitative* - numeric, expresses amounts or magnitudes
- *Qualitative (Categorical)* - labels or groups; expresses kinds or categories

**Quantitative Data**
- Represents numerical quantities on which arithmetic operations produces meaningful results
- Answers the questions, how many? Or how much?
- *Discrete* - countable whole numbers
- *Continuous* - any value in a range

**Qualitative Data**
- Represents categories, labels, or attributes
- Numbers assigned to categories (e.g. 1 = Male, 2 = Female) are codes, not quantities

**Discrete Variables**
- Values are countable integers
- No meaningful values exists between consecutive whole numbers

**Continuous Variables**
- Values can take any real number within a range, limited only by measurement precision

**Nominal Variables**
- Categories with no natural order
- One category is not greater than or less than the other

**Ordinal Variables**
- Categories with a meaningful rank order but the intervals between ranks are not guaranteed equal

**Scales of Measurement**
- *Ratio*: true zero
- *Interval*: no true zero
- *Ordinal*: categories + meaningful order 
- *Nominal*: no order

**Nominal**:
- The lowest level of measurement
- Data are classified into mutually exclusive categories with no natural order or magnitude
- Categories are distinct and exhaustive: every observation belongs to exactly one category
- No ranking - no category is greater than or less than the other
- No arithmetic: adding, subtracting, or averaging categories is meaningless
- Only valid operation: frequency, count

**Ordinal**:
- Categories that have a meaningful rand but the distances between ranks are not equal or known
- Categories are ordered - rand comparisons are valid
- Differences between ranks are not equal
- No arithmetic on raw values - median + percentiles

**Interval Scale**:
- Has all ordinal properties plus equal, meaningful distances between values
- It has no true zero point - zero does not mean complete absence
- Mean and standard deviation are valid

**Ratio Scale**:
- Caries all interval properties
- True zero exists - zero means the complete absence of the measured quantity
- Ratios are meaningful 
- The most powerful scale - all statistical operations are valid
