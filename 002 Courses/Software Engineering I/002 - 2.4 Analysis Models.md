## System Analysis

**Systems Engineering**: a broader discipline focusing on entire systems, constituted by people, processes, data, hardware, software, and its interactions. Focuses on how to design, develop, and manage complex systems over their entire life cycle.

**System Analysis:** understand and empathize with client's needs to
systematically analyze data flow, transformation, storage, and
output in context to the business needs. What is the problem, what do the users need, and what are the exact requirements for a successful solution?

**System Analysts**: Responsible for analyzing the current system and identifying areas for improvement to support the continuous operation of the business.

## Case Modeling

**Unified Modeling Language**: a standardized graphical notation (diagrams)for modeling software systems to describe, specify, design, document, and communicate the architecture of software systems.

**Use Case Diagram**: visually presents the behaviors and services of a proposed system that provides value to the users. Defines the **functional requirements** of the system from the **user's perspective**.

**Use Case Scenario**: represents the standard flow of events and the alternative paths to describe the variations.

## Data Dictionary

**Data Abstraction**: simplification of complex systems in understandable and manageable representations through the hiding of the low-level details of data and shifting the focus towards its essential properties. Showing only the essential information to the user while hiding the complex, underlying details of how that information is stored or processed.

**Data Abstraction Levels**:
1. External Model (End-users) (Abstraction is high here)
2. Conceptual Model (Designers)
3. Internal Model (DBMS)
4. Physical Model (Physical Independence) (Abstraction is low here)

**Data Dictionary**: a repository describing and defining the logical data structures that are meaningful to the daily operations of a business.

## Data Flow Diagram

**Data Modeling**: used to reduce complexities in database design and to ensure that the database data is accurate, consistent, and can be managed efficiently. The process of **creating a visual plan or blueprint for how an organization's data will be stored, managed, and accessed.**

**Data Conservation**: analysts should understand the workflow and processes to accurately apply data conservation. Refers to the policies, practices, and technologies used to keep data available, safe, and accurate over long periods of time. 

**Data Flow Diagram**: visual representation of information flow in a system and issued to communicate technical information more easily with stakeholders.

**Data Flow Diagram**:
1. Level 0: Context Diagram
2. Level 1: Diagram 0
3. Level 2: Diagram 1

**Level 0: Context Diagram**: contains only one process (the system itself) and the entities that interact with it; data stores are hidden. 

**Level 1: Diagram 0**: Highlights the main modules of a system

**Level 2: Diagram 1**: Shows the granular sub-processes of a system. 

## Entity-Relationship Diagram

**Data Model**: a simple and abstract representation on how to structure and organize a more complex real-world object to meet specific business requirements. 

**Data Modeling**: reduce complexities in database design and to ensure that the database data is accurate, consistent, and can be managed efficiently.

**Entity-Relationship Model**: a conceptual approach used to visualize the structures if business databases as abstract data types linked by relationships, expressing dependencies. 

**Entities** - defined as tables that hold specific information.
**Attributes** - associated with an entity and having legal values.
**Relationships** - the associations or interactions between entities.

**Entity-Relationship Diagram**: a diagram visualizing any ER models.
1. Chen Notation
2. Barker's Notation
3. Crow's Foot Notation
4. UML Notation

**Crow's Foot Notation**: an ERD Notation with a distinctive "crows foot" notation depicting cardinality in relationships, gaining popularity over the more verbose Chen Notation.

**Cardinality**: specifies the minimum and maximum number of instances of one entity that can be related to one instance of another entity. 

**Modality**:
1. Mandatory Relationship
2. Optional Relationship

**Mandatory Relationship**: specifies that for every occurrence of Entity A there must exist an Entity B, or vice versa

**Optional Relationship**: specifies that it is not necessary for every entity occurrence to participate in the relationship

## Summary

◦ **Systems Analysis** aims to understand current systems and identify areas for improvements.
◦ **Use Case Modeling** creates a high-level representation of the actors in relationship to the use cases of a system.
◦ **Data Dictionary** describes and defines the logical data structures that represent a system.
◦ **Data Flow Diagram** focuses on mapping what data is required for a specific process initiated by an entity and where to store or retrieve it.
◦ **Entity-Relationship Diagram** shows the entity’s attributes and its relationships to other entities.
