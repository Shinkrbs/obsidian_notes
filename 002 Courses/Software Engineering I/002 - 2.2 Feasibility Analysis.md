## Project Initiation

**Planning Phase**: Sets the foundation of a project by establishing the scope, objectives, resources, timeline, opportunities, and risks

**Project Proposal**: Provides the overview to persuade the decision-makers and funders to approve and finance the project.

**Project Initiation Driving Forces**:
1. Resolve a **problem**
2. Conform (comply) to a **directive**
3. Respond to an **opportunity**

## STEEPLE Analysis

**STEEPLE Analysis**: Used to provide insights into whether the project aligns with *external realities/factors* that might prevent the project from moving forward.

**S - Social**
**T - Technological**
**E - Economic**
**E - Environmental**
**P - Political**
**L - Legal**
**E - Ethical**

*Social*: Involves market research to analyze changing cultural and demographic trends.

*Technological*: Analyzes technological advancements and trends to help improve market positioning.

*Economic*: Analyzes economic trends such as inflation, interest rates, taxes, and recession, employment and the job market.

*Environmental*: Analyzes potential impacts to the environment such as energy efficiency and pollution, as well as its positive impacts. 

*Political*: Analyzes prevailing and upcoming political shifts that can greatly affect other areas of feasibility.

*Legal*: Analyzes legal frameworks that governs the market and factors such as public health, safety, employment, fair competition, and data privacy. 

*Ethical*: Analyzes local and global issues pertaining values, morality, and social responsibility to better position the venture.

## Feasibility Analysis

 **Feasibility Analysis**: Determines the viability of a project through the *assessment of the internal capabilities and resources* such as cost, time resources, risks, and benefits to evaluate the potential and whether it has a reasonable chance of success.

**Should we build this new software, and can we actually do it successfully?**

**Feasibility Analysis in Simpler terms**: An early-stage evaluation to determine if a proposed project is viable (practicable, achievable, and worthwhile) before a company commits significant time, money, and resources to the development process. 

Feasibility Analysis identifies all the **risks of failure.**

**Feasibility Analysis should cover the following**:
1. Cost-Benefit Analysis
2. Financial Analysis
3. Conclusions
4. Recommendations

**Areas of Feasibility**:
1. Operational
2. Technical
3. Resource
4. Schedule
5. Legal
6. Economic

**Operational Feasibility**: Assess the **fitness of the proposed solution** to the existing operations and goals of the stakeholders to avoid workflow disruption.

**Will it work for the users?**

**Technical Feasibility**: Determine if the project can be completed within the available *technology, skills and timeline.*

**Can we build it?**

**Resource Feasibility:** Assess the availability of money, personnel, and materials to determine if the project is worth the time and effort.

**Do we have what we need?**

**Schedule Feasibility:** Assess all the tasks and the sufficiency of the time frames, considering the potential risks and delays.

**Can we finish on time?**

**Legal Feasibility:** Evaluate the laws, regulations, and other legal implications to *avoid potential legal issue or liabilities that may arise from the project*.

**Is it allowed? Is it legal?**

**Economic Feasibility:** Assess the projected costs, revenues, and benefits of the project, and compares them to the expected costs and potential returns.

**Is it financially worth it?**

## Financial Metrics

**Financial Metrics**:
1. Payback Period
2. Net-Present Value
3. Cash-Flow Analysis
4. Break-Even Analysis

**Payback Period**: Tells the project's break-even time. The shorter the time, the less risky the investment. How long will it take to get the initial money back.
- Calculate when the proposed benefits are strong and convincing.

**Net-Present Value**: The total value of all future profits (and costs) from a project, converted back into today's money. "Time Value Money" which recognizes that money today is worth more than the same amount in the future. 
- Calculate when the payback period is long, or the cost is too high.

**Cash-Flow Analysis**: A look at all the money coming into a company and all the money leaving it over a specific period. How much cash is moving in and out of the business, and when?
- Calculate when the proposed project is expensive.

**Break-Even Analysis**: Determines the point at which total revenue exactly equals total costs. It sets the minimum target for sales. Everything sold above the break-even point is pure profit. How many units must be sold just to cover all the costs?
- Project must be justified by the cost and not with the benefits.

**Financial Metrics**: The financial numbers (like NPV and Payback Period) show the project _could_ make money, but those numbers are just a guess until you add an experienced team to manage the money carefully and handle problems. These estimates show how confident the founders are, but they also highlight how easily the project could fail if they can't secure necessary funding from investors.

**Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Are the gains we expect from this decision worth more than the money and resources will cost us?

**Cash Outflow**: the total anticipated costs of a software development project, projected over a specific period.

**Cash Inflow**: the total projected cash generated by a software project over a specific period.
