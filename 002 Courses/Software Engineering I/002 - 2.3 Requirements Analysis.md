## Requirements 

**Analysis Phase**: a detailed exploration of the proposed project by gathering, documenting, and analyzing the user needs and its requirements.

**Requirement**: a need or condition that must be met by a software and is typically specified by the client or customer. States how a system should behave or operate, without necessarily knowing the details of how it is implemented. 

**Types of Requirements**:
1. User 
2. System

**Types of System Requirements**:
1. Functional
2. Non-Functional Requirements

**User Requirements**: statements describing the goals and needs of the stakeholders (clients), expressed in non-technical language in the perspective of end-users.

**System Requirements**: detailed technical descriptions of how the software will fulfill/meet the user requirements.

**Functional Requirements**: Define the tasks and functions that a system must perform and are used to define technical specifications. What must the system be able to do. 

**Non-functional Requirements**: describe the behavioral properties that a system must have and are used to determine usability, reliability, performance, and other non-functional properties of the system. Defines how well, under what conditions, or in what manner the system must perform its functions.

## Requirements Gathering

**Requirements Gathering**: the process of identifying, documenting, and validating the needs of the stakeholders.

**Information Gathering**: involves researching and gathering data from both internal and external sources, to draw conclusions and make decisions. 

**Goals of Information Gathering**:
1. Understand system constriants
2. Understand system functions
3. Understand system procedures
4. Develop requirements and models for the new system

**Steps in Information Gathering**
1. Gather background materials
2. Establish data gathering objectives
3. Determine the data gathering techniques to use
4. Schedule an initial site visit
5. Identify contact persons
6. Assign to data gathering teams
7. Schedule data gathering activities
8. Identify deliverables

**Methods for Information Gathering**:
1. Distribution of Questionnaires
2. Interview Stakeholders
3. Observe and Document the Processes (Site Visit)
4. Review Existing Documents
5. Joint Application Design (JAD) Session

**Distribution of Questionnaires**: allows the study of attitudes, behavior, beliefs, and characteristics of the stakeholders affected by the current and proposed project.

**Interview Stakeholders**: Interviews are directed conversations in a Q&A format.
- Open-ended questions
- Close-ended questions

**Observe and Document Processes (Site Visit)**: Helps understand business functions through quick walk through on the office layout, equipment, and general workflow.

**Review Existing Documents**: Identify requirements that may not be covered in the interview and help discover discrepancies and redundant processes, or verify whether assumptions are correct. 
- Internal Sources - Documents found within the organization
- External Sources - Documents online from organizations and companies of the same category in the industry.

**Joint Application Design Session**: an iterative process of gathering and analyzing business requirements to develop a solution, involving some representatives of the stakeholders.

## Requirements Determination

**Requirements Development Methodology**: A structured process for defining, organizing, validating, and managing the requirements of a system. The plan for ensuring that the development team and the client clearly agree on what is being built before any code is written.
