## Requirements 

**Analysis Phase**: a detailed exploration of the proposed project by gathering, documenting, and analyzing the user needs and its requirements.

**Requirement**: a need or condition that must be met by a software and is typically specified by the client or customer. States how a system should behave or operate, without necessarily knowing the details of how it is implemented. 

**Types of Requirements**:
1. User 
2. System

**Types of System Requirements**:
1. Functional
2. Non-Functional Requirements

**User Requirements**: statements describing the goals and needs of the stakeholders (clients), expressed in non-technical language in the perspective of end-users.

**System Requirements**: detailed technical descriptions of how the software will fulfill/meet the user requirements.

**Functional Requirements**: Define the tasks and functions that a system must perform and are used to define technical specifications. What must the system be able to do. 

**Non-functional Requirements**: describe the behavioral properties that a system must have and are used to determine usability, reliability, performance, and other non-functional properties of the system. Defines how well, under what conditions, or in what manner the system must perform its functions.

## Requirements Gathering

**Requirements Gathering**: the process of identifying, documenting, and validating the needs of the stakeholders.

**Information Gathering**: involves researching and gathering data from both internal and external sources, to draw conclusions and make decisions. 

**Goals of Information Gathering**:
1. Understand system constriants
2. Understand system functions
3. Understand system procedures
4. Develop requirements and models for the new system

**Steps in Information Gathering**
1. Gather background materials
2. Establish data gathering objectives
3. Determine the data gathering techniques to use
4. Schedule an initial site visit
5. Identify contact persons
6. Assign to data gathering teams
7. Schedule data gathering activities
8. Identify deliverables

**Methods for Information Gathering**:
1. Distribution of Questionnaires
2. Interview Stakeholders
3. Observe and Document the Processes (Site Visit)
4. Review Existing Documents
5. Joint Application Design (JAD) Session

**Distribution of Questionnaires**: allows the study of attitudes, behavior, beliefs, and characteristics of the stakeholders affected by the current and proposed project.

**Interview Stakeholders**: Interviews are directed conversations in a Q&A format.
- Open-ended questions
- Close-ended questions

**Observe and Document Processes (Site Visit)**: Helps understand business functions through quick walk through on the office layout, equipment, and general workflow.

**Review Existing Documents**: Identify requirements that may not be covered in the interview and help discover discrepancies and redundant processes, or verify whether assumptions are correct. 
- Internal Sources - Documents found within the organization
- External Sources - Documents online from organizations and companies of the same category in the industry.

**Joint Application Design Session**: an iterative process of gathering and analyzing business requirements to develop a solution, involving some representatives of the stakeholders.

## Requirements Determination

**Requirements Development Methodology**: A structured process for defining, organizing, validating, and managing the requirements of a system. The plan for ensuring that the development team and the client clearly agree on what is being built before any code is written.

**Requirements Development Methodology**:
1. Identify Stakeholders
2. Determine the Requirements
3. Check for Completeness
4. Document the Requirements
5. Review and Sign-off
6. Managing Changes

**Requirements Determination**: to deliver the client's full requirements and expectations leading to the development of a product or service; and must be adaptable to the unique needs of the clients.

**Primary Roles in RDP**:
1. Determiners: Ensures the clients needs are accurately conveyed.
2. Client: Provide the specific business knowledge in describing the need for a product or service.
3. Recipients: Use the requirements to build a product or service.

**Requirements Determination Process**:
1. Planning and Management
2. Obtain
3. Understand
4. Validate
5. Evaluation of the RDP

**Planning and Management**: refers to the development of specific approaches to project management.

**Obtain**: the collection of relevant information from which client's requirements are determined after. 

**Understand**: refers to the analysis of the collected information and grasping of the implications of the business requirements.

**Validate**: refers to the two-way confirmation of the implications of the requirements with the client.

**Evaluation of the RDP**: refers to the assessment of the execution and results of the requirements determination process. 

**Ending the RDP**: RDP practically never ends but must be formally agreed with all the stakeholders when all the requirements are already sufficiently stable or when changes become increasingly risky.

**Value of RDP**:
1. Reduced Cost
2. Improved time to market
3. Direct stakeholder value
4. Increases efficiency
5. Reduced risks

## Requirements Analysis

**Requirements Analysis**: refers to the software engineering task that bridges the gap between the system-level software allocation and the software design. The mental work and documentation phase that ensures the technical team builds the right thing, and that the right thing is technically feasible.

**Areas of Effort**:
1. Recognize the problem
2. Evaluate and synthesize
3. Conceptualize the model
4. Define the specification
5. Review and iterate

**Objectives of Analysis**:
1. Describe requirements
2. Establish the basis of software design
3. Define the related set of requirements for validation and testing

**Configuration Management**: refers to the set of procedures that tracks software requirements, design modules, code base, tests files, and documentations. A system for tracking and controlling every single change  and ensuring that the system is always in a known, consistent, and correct state.

**Elements of Analysis Models**
1. Data Dictionary
2. Data Object Description
3. Process Specification
4. Control Specification

**Data Dictionary**: a repository of all data objects consumed and produced by the software.

**Data Object Description**: stores and provides the complete knowledge of all the data objects used in a software.
- *Entity-Relationship Diagram* is a graphical representation of entities and their relationships in a database structure.

**Data Model**: a simple and abstract representation on how to structure and organize complex real-life objects to meet specific business requirements.

**Process Specification**: any method used to document, analyze, and explain the logic used to process input data and generate output data.
- *Data Flow Diagram* is used in systems design as a visual representation of the information flow in a system and is issued  to communicate the technical information more easily to all the stakeholders.

**Control Specification**: represents the behavior of the software
- *State Transition Diagrams* shows various modes of states and transitions of a software from one state to another.
