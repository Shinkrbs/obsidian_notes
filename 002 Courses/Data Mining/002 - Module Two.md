# 2.1 Understanding Big Data

## 2.1.1 Defining Big Data

Tags: #Size

**Big Data**: is a term used to describe the massive volumes of digital data generated, collected, and processed.  It describes data that is either moving too quickly, is simply too large, or is too complex to be stored, processed, or analyzed with traditional data storage and analytics applications. 

Examples of big data:
- Data generated by postings to social media accounts such as, Facebook and Twitter.
- Ratings given to products on e-commerce sites like Amazon marketplace.

***Size is only one characteristics that define big data. Other criteria include the speed of generated data and the variety of data collected and stored.***

## 2.1.2 Big Data Characteristics

Tags:  #Volume #Variety #Velocity #Veracity

Characteristics of big data change how data is collected, transmitted, stored and accessed. 

**Volume**: describes the amount of data transported and stored. International Data Corporation (IDC) experts predict that data volume will increase at a compound annual growth of 23% over the next five years. 

**Variety**: describes the many forms of data can take. A significant contributor to big data is unstructured data, such as video, images, text documents.

**Velocity**: describes the rate at which this data is generated.

**Veracity**: the process of preventing inaccurate data from spoiling your data sets. Increased veracity in the collection of data can reduce the amount of data cleaning that is required.
